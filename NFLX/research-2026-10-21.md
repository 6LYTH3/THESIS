# NFLX — Netflix, Inc.

> Historical research snapshot, preserved from the October 21 pass. Superseded by README.md and the November 11 primary-source deep dive. In particular, low physical capex is not proof of low content investment, 2026 reported FCF includes a termination fee, and older buyback figures/conclusions should not be used without the corrections in the current report.

**Sector:** Technology / Media & Entertainment (Streaming)
**Last updated:** 2026-10-21 (updated for Q3 2026 earnings call)

## Summary
Netflix is the leading global subscription streaming platform. The company is a
cash-generation machine: operating cash flow has grown from $0.4B (FY2021) to $10.1B
(FY2025) and $12.0B TTM (Jun '26), with free cash flow of $9,461M (FY2025)
and $11,152M TTM. Capital allocation has shifted from net deleveraging (early years)
to aggressive share repurchases ($6.0B in FY2023, $6.3B in FY2024, $9.2B in
FY2025, $10.0B TTM) with no dividend. Capital expenditures remain tiny relative to cash
flow (<7% of CFO), the hallmark of an asset-light content business.

The October 20, 2026 Q3 call confirmed strong execution but introduced near-term
monetization risk: revenue was approximately $12.9B (+12% year over year), operating
margin was 34.6%, diluted EPS was $0.87, and Q3 free cash flow was $2.9B. Management
reduced its 2026 advertising-revenue expectation to $2.7B from approximately $3B and
FY2026 FCF guidance to $12.0B from $12.5B. The core thesis still works, but the ad ramp
is taking longer than planned.

## Latest Earnings Call — Q3 2026 (October 20, 2026)
- **Results:** Revenue ~$12.9B, +12% year over year (+11% FX-neutral); operating income
  ~$4.5B, +37%; operating margin 34.6%; diluted EPS $0.87 versus $0.59 in Q3 2025.
- **Cash returns:** Q3 FCF was $2.9B; Netflix repurchased $4.8B of stock. Gross debt
  ended the quarter at approximately $15.3B after a $1B bond issuance for maturities.
- **Forward guidance:** Q4 revenue $13.4B (+12% year over year) and 26.0% operating
  margin. FY2026 revenue guidance is $51.1B, operating margin 31.5%, and FCF about $12.0B.
- **Advertising:** 2026 ad revenue is expected to grow more than 80% but reach only
  ~$2.7B because product rollouts and partner integrations have taken longer. Netflix
  plans to launch the ad tier in 15 additional countries in 2027.
- **Engagement:** Global and U.S. churn improved year over year. India was particularly
  strong, with Q3 view hours up 24% and revenue up nearly 20% FX-neutral.
- **Read-through:** Good operating quarter, but the lower ad and FCF outlook makes the
  earnings quality less clean than the headline EPS beat.

## Business & Financials
- Content-driven subscription model; CFO scaled with content spend
  normalization and pricing increases.
- CapEx is structurally small (<7% of CFO) because content is amortized, not
  capitalized as fixed assets.
- Net income: $5,116M (FY2021) → $4,492M (FY2022) → $5,408M (FY2023) →
  $8,712M (FY2024) → $10,981M (FY2025).
- CFO growth: +259% FY2023, +38% FY2025.

## Bull case
- FCF conversion is exceptional: $9,461M FCF (FY2025), $11,152M TTM.
- Minimal capex and near-zero acquisition cadence keep the cash funnel intact.
- Q3 showed pricing power and improving churn, while India and other international
  markets demonstrate that a stronger content slate can still accelerate growth.
- Advertising can become a meaningful incremental revenue stream if delayed product
  integrations are fixed and the 2027 country rollout succeeds.

## Bear case
- Buybacks have grown every year since FY2023 ($6.0B → $6.3B → $9.2B) and now
  consume roughly 97% of FCF each year, with no dividend and no meaningful
  cushion left after acquisitions.
- Any content spend re-acceleration or competitive price war compresses the
  CFO−CapEx gap.
- No dividend and buyback pace that tracks FCF 1:1 means equity count
  reduction is the only shareholder return channel.
- The ad business is behind its aggressive roadmap: the 2026 target fell to $2.7B and
  management acknowledged rollout and partner-integration delays.
- Q4 margin is guided to 26.0% and FY2026 FCF to $12.0B; U.S. growth is not as strong
  as management wants.

## FCF & Capital Allocation View

Analysis pass 2026-10-07.

1. **The FCF inflection is the real story.** FY2021→FY2023 CFO went $0.4B →
   $2.0B → $7.3B as the pandemic-era content budget bloat (FY2021-22) was
   normalized. FY2025 FCF $9,461M, TTM already $11,152M, FCF margin ~21% TTM.
   Mature cash machine, not growth-at-all-costs. CapEx stayed <7% of CFO
   throughout, so FCF growth came from operations, not capital discipline.
2. **The buyback pivot is the key allocation signal.** Zero buybacks for
   years, then $6.0B (FY2023) → $6.3B (FY2024) → $9.2B (FY2025), a 46% jump
   in one year. That implies management believes FCF is structural, not
   cyclical — i.e. the content cost base is now predictable.
3. **They return ~100% of FCF.** FY2025: $9,173M buybacks vs $9,461M FCF
   while *also* deleveraging $1,833M. TTM buybacks ($10,0B) exceed TTM FCF
   slightly; stock issuance (ESPP/RSU-related) fills the rest. No balance
   sheet cushion being built.
4. **No dividend is deliberate.** With a shrinking share count, buybacks beat
   a dividend for EPS accretion and can be dialed down in a down quarter
   with zero optics problem.
5. **Acquisitions near zero since FY2022.** Full pivot to own content +
   scale; cash goes to equity, not assets.
6. **The risk is the 1:1 payout.** If ad revenue or pricing slows, CFO dips
   and buybacks either get cut (ugly signal) or are funded by debt (FY2024
   +$1,394M net issuance proves they will do it).

**Bottom line:** NFLX has quietly converted from a growth story into a
capital-return story. The buyback ramp is management's own vote of
confidence in the $10B+ FCF run-rate, and it is the single most important
item in the table.

## Conclusion
**Attractive, but with a higher execution bar after Q3.** Netflix delivered strong Q3
profitability, improving churn, pricing power, and roughly $12B of FY2026 FCF guidance.
The main risk is monetization execution: advertising is growing rapidly but below its
initial target, while buybacks continue to consume nearly all excess FCF. The 2027 ad
rollout and U.S. content execution are the key confirmation points.
