# STX — Seagate Technology
# Historical research snapshot — superseded by README.md

**Sector:** Technology — Storage / HDD
**Last updated:** 2026-11-03

## Summary
Seagate is a leading HDD supplier benefiting from AI/cloud storage demand and
HAMR density gains. Fiscal Q1 FY2027 delivered $4.23B revenue, 57.8% adjusted
gross margin and $1.48B free cash flow, but nearline shipments disappointed
expectations and Q2 guidance fell short of some investors' higher expectations.
On November 2, shares fell 7.88% to $593.76 alongside a broad chip/storage selloff,
not an identified new Seagate profit warning. My view is neutral: the lower
price improves prospective returns, but December shipment execution and the
durability of pricing remain the key tests.

## Fiscal Q1 2027 earnings release (2026-10-27)
- **Results:** Revenue was **$4.23B**; non-GAAP gross margin **57.8%** and EPS **$7.68**, with operating cash flow of **$1.69B** and free cash flow of **$1.48B**.
- **Guide:** Q2 revenue is guided to **$4.8B ±$100M** and non-GAAP EPS to **$9.20 ±$0.20**, indicating that nearline demand and pricing are still strengthening.
- **Strategic message:** Management described a multi-year storage-growth phase with long-term customer visibility, supported by HAMR/Mozaic areal-density gains, supply discipline and value-based pricing.
- **Capital allocation:** Seagate retired $1.2B of debt during the quarter and another $750M after quarter-end, taking gross debt to $1.6B. It returned $568M to shareholders and raised the quarterly dividend about 3% to $0.76.
- **Investment read-through:** Results support the near-term bull case but do not establish how long shortage pricing will last; subsequent reporting highlights shipment and investor-expectation misses despite strong headline results.

## Business & Financials
- **Supply discipline:** CFO (Citi conf., Sept 13, 2026) says the supply/demand
  gap is *widening*, not narrowing; POs cover 4–5 quarters, long-term exabyte
  agreements 2–3 years; nearline capacity substantially allocated through
  2028.
- **HAMR leadership:** shipping 2nd-gen HAMR, 3rd gen in a few quarters;
  transitioning 30TB → 40TB, qualifying 50TB within ~a year; targeting ≥25%
  annual exabyte growth over 2–3 years; vertically integrated heads/media.
- **FQ3 2026** (qtr ended Apr 3, 2026): revenue $3.11B; non-GAAP gross margin
  47.0%; non-GAAP EPS $4.10 vs ~$3.51 est (beat); FCF $953M; $641M debt
  retired; FCF now directed to buybacks + dividend.
- Capex guided 4–6% of revenue; opex ~$300M/qtr; street median 12-mo target
  ~$1,150 (range $700–$1,600).

## Moat & what breaks it
Moat = (1) oligopoly + supply discipline (~40% unit share, nearline sold out /
allocated through 2027-2028, 52+ wk lead times, nearline prices +35-46% since
Sep 2025), (2) HAMR lead (only mass producer; Mozaic 4+ 44TB qualified with
two hyperscalers, revenue to 75% of top cloud customers; density rises with
minimal BOM change = structural cost-per-TB edge), (3) switching costs from
hyperscaler qualification + 2-3 yr build-to-order exabyte contracts.

Ranked moat-breakers:
1. **Toshiba capacity doubling (FY2027)** — the moat is really a *disciplined
   supply* story; 2x AI DC HDD output + ~30% share target would unwind
   pricing power and 50%+ gross margins together (fast de-rating).
2. **HAMR lead closing** — WDC HAMR mass production 2027; Seagate's
   cost-per-TB edge is the one compounding moat element; if WDC/Toshiba
   catch up, STX becomes just one of 2-3 equivalent commodity makers.
3. **AI capex digestion (anytime)** — a demand breaker, not a moat breaker;
   a capex pause hits utilization, pricing and multiples before the moat
   erodes.
4. **QLC SSD erosion (2028+)** — enterprise NVMe ~16x nearline $/TB today
   (vs ~6x in 2025), so substitution is far off, but NAND cost curves are
   the structural clock on HDD.

## Bull case
- Sold out through 2028; the two-firm (STX/WDC) supply structure has held
  pricing power for 3 years and may reassert it.
- HAMR generational lead widens cost-per-TB advantage over Toshiba/WDC over
  the next 2–3 years.
- Debt effectively retired; cash machine funding buybacks at $200B+ market cap.

## Bear case
- Toshiba doubling AI data-center capacity breaks the oligopoly discipline;
  a supply glut would erase pricing power and the scarcity premium in the
  multiple.
- Valuation still rich after −10% (high-50s/low-60s earnings on last prints,
  even at $849); any guidance tone-down on pricing would compound the
  de-rating.
- AI data-center capex digestion would hit the very demand driving nearline
  exabytes.

## Valuation
**Updated November 2, 2026:** $593.76 close and approximately $136B market cap.
Annualizing Q2 adjusted EPS guidance ($9.20 × 4 = $36.80) gives approximately
16.1x earnings at this price. This is a quarterly run-rate illustration, not a
full-year forecast or normalized-cycle valuation. TheStreet reports Morgan
Stanley's FY2027 EPS estimate of $40.84, implying about 14.5x; this is one
analyst's forecast, not consensus. The price is materially lower than in the
historical discussion below; do not use the old multiples as current valuation.

Historical October 2 valuation:
~$215B market cap pre-drop at $945.57; ~$191B at the $848.99 close. The
stock's re-rating to ~68x was driven by the shortage, so its downside is tied
to the credibility of Toshiba's expansion: if it lands in FY2027 as reported,
the scarcity premium is partly impaired; if HAMR scale-out keeps STX costs
well below Toshiba's, STX absorbs the share loss at better margins than the
industry average.

## Conclusion
**Neutral / cautious hold; valuation is more interesting after the selloff.**
November 2 looks primarily like sector selling rather than demonstrated new
company-specific deterioration. However, weak sequential shipment growth and
elevated expectations explain why strong earnings alone have not supported the
shares. Watch December exabyte growth, HAMR qualifications, pricing versus costs,
and customer demand; do not assume either a broken thesis or a confirmed bottom.
