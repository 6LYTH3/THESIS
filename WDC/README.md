# WDC — Western Digital Corporation

**Sector:** Technology / Data Storage
**Last updated:** 2026-10-23

## Summary
Western Digital reported fiscal Q1 2027 results on October 22, 2026, with revenue of $4.381 billion, up 55% year over year, and non-GAAP EPS of $4.64, up 161%. Revenue and adjusted EPS exceeded reported consensus of approximately $4.14 billion and $4.08, respectively. Results were driven by strong cloud/storage demand, higher pricing, and operating leverage. Management's Q2 outlook is substantially stronger again, but the valuation and expectations now depend on continued tight supply, pricing, and successful technology transitions.

## Business & Financials
- Q1 FY27 revenue was $4.381 billion (+55% YoY; +17% QoQ).
- GAAP gross margin was 57.8%; non-GAAP gross margin was 58.1%, versus 43.9% a year earlier.
- Non-GAAP operating margin reached 49.1%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $4.64 versus $1.78 in Q1 FY26.
- Operating cash flow was $1.604 billion and free cash flow was $1.377 billion. WD repurchased $1.001 billion of stock during the quarter.

## Bull case
- Q2 FY27 guidance calls for $5.1 billion of revenue +/- $100 million, 60%–61% non-GAAP gross margin, and $5.80 +/- $0.20 non-GAAP EPS.
- Robust exabyte growth, improved pricing, cloud demand, and HAMR/product transitions could extend the earnings upcycle.
- The board raised the quarterly dividend 17% to $0.175 per share and continued significant buybacks.

## Bear case
- The earnings base and margins are unusually elevated, leaving the stock vulnerable to any pricing, supply, cloud-capex, or execution disappointment.
- Revenue is concentrated in cloud customers, while technology transitions and manufacturing/supply-chain execution remain important risks.
- GAAP net income attributable to common shareholders fell 44% sequentially, partly reflecting the prior quarter's non-operating gain; comparisons can therefore be noisy.

## Valuation
The report was a clear operational beat, but the forward Q2 guide implies that much of the improvement is already expected by investors. At current elevated earnings expectations, valuation should be assessed on normalized cash flow rather than peak-cycle EPS alone.

## Conclusion
**Neutral-to-constructive:** the quarter and Q2 guide were excellent, strengthening the fundamental thesis. I would avoid chasing a sharp post-earnings move until it is clear that pricing and margins can remain durable through the HDD technology transition and a broader cycle.
