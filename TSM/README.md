# TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company

**Sector:** Semiconductors / Foundry Manufacturing
**Last updated:** 2026-10-20

## Summary
TSMC is a contract manufacturer for advanced logic chips: customers such as Apple, Nvidia, AMD, and many others design chips, while TSMC turns those designs into physical silicon wafers and packaged chips. It pioneered the “pure-play foundry” model, meaning it generally does not design or sell competing branded chips, allowing it to manufacture for multiple chip designers. In 2025, TSMC served 534 customers, made 12,682 products using 305 process technologies, and generated US$122.4B of revenue. Its competitive advantage is the combination of leading process technology, manufacturing yields, scale, and an ecosystem of design tools and packaging services.

## Business & Financials
- Customers send TSMC chip designs; TSMC fabricates them in semiconductor fabs using processes such as 3nm, 5nm, and 7nm, then provides advanced packaging and testing-related services.
- Its chips power AI and high-performance-computing servers, smartphones, cars, PCs, networking equipment, game consoles, and IoT devices.
- 2025 revenue was US$122.42B and net income was US$55.21B; 74% of wafer revenue came from 7nm-and-more-advanced technologies.
- TSMC shipped 15.0 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers in 2025 and had annual capacity above 17 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers.
- The company is headquartered in Taiwan and is expanding manufacturing in the United States, Japan, and Germany, while continuing to put most leading-edge capacity in Taiwan.

## Bull case
- AI demand requires huge volumes of increasingly advanced chips, and TSMC is a critical supplier to much of the AI ecosystem.
- Its technology lead, high yields, scale, and customer ecosystem make it difficult and expensive for competitors to replicate.
- The fabless model creates broad customer diversification while avoiding direct competition with customers.

## Bear case
- A Taiwan Strait crisis or broader geopolitical conflict could disrupt its most important manufacturing base and global chip supply.
- Building leading-edge fabs requires enormous capital spending, while process delays, poor yields, or weaker chip demand could hurt returns.
- Intel Foundry, Samsung, and other manufacturers could eventually narrow TSMC’s technology or capacity advantage.
- Customer concentration and cyclical smartphone, PC, and semiconductor demand remain meaningful risks.

## Valuation
TSMC is best understood as the infrastructure provider—the “toll road”—for advanced computing rather than as a typical chip-brand company. The investment case depends on whether future AI and computing growth justifies the high capital intensity and premium generally assigned to its technology leadership. A valuation should use current ADR price, earnings, free cash flow, and planned capital expenditure; those figures are not included here because this note is focused on explaining the business.

## Conclusion
**Attractive business, but geopolitically exposed.** TSMC manufactures much of the world’s most advanced logic silicon and benefits from the growth of AI and increasingly powerful electronics. Its moat is unusually strong, but Taiwan concentration, massive capital requirements, semiconductor cyclicality, and competition mean the stock still needs to be bought at a sensible valuation.
