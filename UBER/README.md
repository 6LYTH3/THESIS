# UBER — Uber Technologies

**Sector:** Technology / Internet Platforms (Mobility, Delivery, Freight)
**Last updated:** 2026-10-09

## Summary
Uber operates the world's largest mobility and delivery marketplace, with FY2025 revenue of $52.0B (up 18.3%) across Mobility ($29.7B), Delivery ($17.2B), and Freight ($5.1B). The company has fully converted to scale: FY2025 net income of $10.1B, OCF of $10.1B, and FCF of $9.8B (TTM FCF $10.1B at an 18.3% margin), with minimal capex (~$0.3B/yr) in an asset-light model. With FY2026 consensus revenue of ~$57.9B (+11%) and EPS estimates of ~$3.36, the story has shifted from growth to cash flow. At a $143.5B market cap, the stock trades at 15.3x trailing earnings and 14.2x FCF — a modest multiple for a platform growing ~15-18% with expanding FCF margins.

## Business & Financials
- Revenue: FY2021 $17.5B → FY2022 $31.9B → FY2023 $37.3B → FY2024 $44.0B → FY2025 $52.0B → TTM (Jun '26) $55.2B
- FCF: FY2023 $3.4B → FY2024 $6.9B → FY2025 $9.8B → TTM $10.1B (FY2022 figures distorted by a $7.2B loss on sale of investments)
- Net income: FY2025 $10.1B; TTM $9.6B
- Capex is trivial (~$220-340M/yr, <0.7% of revenue) — FCF tracks OCF closely
- Balance sheet: TTM net debt ~$4.7B (cash & investments $10.0B vs total debt $14.7B)
- Valuation (10/8/26): market cap $143.5B, share price $70.24, ~2.04B shares, P/E 15.3, forward P/E 17.3, P/FCF 14.2

## Bull case
- FCF margin of ~18% is still expanding; every revenue point compounds into ~$0.9B of incremental FCF
- Marketplace network effects and share of ride-hailing/delivery remain dominant globally
- At ~14x FCF with double-digit growth, PEG is well below 1; consensus price target ~$101 implies ~40% upside

## Bear case
- Marketplace take-rate pressure from competition (DoorDash, in-housing, autonomous vehicles)
- Growth decelerating: revenue growth has stepped down from ~18% (FY2025) to ~11% (FY2026E consensus)
- Net debt rising (TTM $4.7B), and FCF could compress if delivery incentives heat up again

## Conclusion
**Neutral-to-attractive.** At 15% required return the DCF implies ~$58/share vs $70.24 market — the stock is fully valued at that hurdle, with the terminal value accounting for ~61% of equity value (typical sensitivity). However, the 15% hurdle is aggressive for a business growing FCF 40%+ over the past two years at 18% margins; at a more standard 13-14% discount rate the shares look fairly-to-cheaply priced. Core risk is FCF margin expansion stalling as delivery competition intensifies.
